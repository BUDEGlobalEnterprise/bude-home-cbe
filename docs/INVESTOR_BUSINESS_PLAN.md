## 1. Executive summary

BUDE HOME CBE is building a mobile-first operating system and trusted discovery marketplace for Paying Guest (PG) accommodation. The product is designed to replace fragmented spreadsheets, paper registers, chat messages, and disconnected payment records with one secure workflow for property owners, staff, and tenants.

PG owners receive tools for property and bed inventory, occupancy, tenant onboarding, recurring rent, deposits, payments, receipts, complaints, staff work, and business reporting. Tenants receive verified discovery, transparent pricing and rules, applications, booking, digital stay records, payments, receipts, and service requests. Only subscribed and eligible properties are visible in the marketplace.

The business begins as a B2B SaaS product funded by PG owners. Tenant usage remains free. Marketplace demand and payment automation increase product value and retention, but the initial business does not depend on advertising or selling personal data.

### Investment proposition

- A large, recurring operational problem in a fragmented accommodation category.
- Subscription revenue with the potential for high gross margin and predictable renewals.
- A focused city-cluster launch that supports cost-efficient field sales and onboarding.
- A two-sided data and distribution advantage: operational supply software improves marketplace accuracy; marketplace demand improves owner acquisition and retention.
- A production plan centered on tenant isolation, payment correctness, KYC privacy, auditability, and reliable operations.
- A proposed **₹2.25 crore pre-seed round** to fund approximately 18 months and reach repeatable multi-city sales evidence.

## 2. The problem

Independent and small-chain PG operators commonly manage a complex recurring business using tools that were not designed for it. Inventory may be in a spreadsheet, tenant documents in folders, rent reminders in messaging applications, payments in bank statements, complaints in personal chats, and occupancy knowledge in one manager's memory.

This creates five expensive problems:

1. **Revenue leakage:** missed rent, unrecorded add-on charges, poor overdue follow-up, and weak payment reconciliation.
2. **Inventory uncertainty:** owners cannot reliably see which beds are vacant, reserved, occupied, under notice, or unavailable.
3. **Operational dependency:** critical knowledge resides with individuals rather than a shared, auditable system.
4. **Tenant friction:** tenants face unclear availability, inconsistent rules, manual paperwork, weak receipt history, and slow complaint resolution.
5. **Trust and compliance exposure:** identity documents, deposits, refunds, and financial records are often handled without adequate access control, retention, or audit evidence.

Generic property-management tools focus on apartments and leases. Hotel systems optimize nightly reservations. ERP systems are powerful but require heavier implementation. PG accommodation requires a purpose-built combination of monthly residency, individual-bed allocation, recurring billing, deposits, owner subscriptions, tenant self-service, and local marketplace discovery.

## 3. The solution

BUDE HOME CBE provides one workflow across the PG lifecycle.

### Owner and staff product

- Organization, property, floor, room, and individual-bed inventory.
- Real-time vacant, held, reserved, occupied, notice, maintenance, and archived states.
- Owner and property-scoped staff permissions.
- Tenant applications, offers, reservation, check-in, room transfer, notice, and checkout.
- Versioned pricing, deposits, facilities, rules, and stay terms.
- Monthly invoices, variable charges, partial/offline/online payments, receipts, credits, and reconciliation.
- Deposit receipt, proposed deductions, approval/dispute, refund, and final statement.
- Complaints, staff assignment, comments, status, SLA, and escalation.
- Occupancy, collections, dues, move-in/out, and service dashboards.
- Subscription, plan limits, and marketplace publication controls.

### Tenant product

- List, map, location, price, sharing, facility, food, and availability discovery.
- Clear property details, photos, prices, deposit, rules, and facilities.
- Application, offer acceptance, booking, and move-in status.
- Secure KYC submission based on a minimal, legally reviewed policy.
- Digital stay details, invoices, payments, receipts, deposits, and refund status.
- Notice, transfer, checkout, complaint, and resolution history.
- Role-aware mobile application with privacy-safe notifications.

### Platform product

- Plan and subscription administration.
- Property publication and moderation.
- Provider, queue, payment-reconciliation, and operational health.
- Time-bound, reason-required, MFA-protected support access.
- Immutable audit history and security monitoring.

## 4. Why now

- India's higher-education system reported approximately **4.33 crore students enrolled in 2021–22**, including 2.07 crore women, demonstrating the scale of one important accommodation-demand segment. This is a demand indicator, not a claim that every student needs a PG. [Source: Ministry of Education, AISHE 2021–22](https://www.education.gov.in/sites/upload_files/mhrd/files/statistics-new/AISHE%20Book_2021-22_4.pdf).
- Digital payment expectations make manual payment confirmation and receipt issuance increasingly costly for both owners and tenants.
- Mobile-first consumers expect availability, terms, payment status, and service history to be accessible without repeated calls or messages.
- Small operators can now access secure managed infrastructure and payment integrations without owning a large internal IT team.
- The category remains operationally fragmented, creating room for a vertical product that combines system-of-record depth with localized distribution.

## 5. Customer and market focus

### 5.1 Primary paying customer

The initial customer is an owner/operator managing approximately 20–300 beds across one to five PG properties. This segment has enough operational pain and monthly cash flow to pay for software, but is often underserved by enterprise property systems.

Buyer personas:

- Owner-operator who manages occupancy and collections personally.
- Small PG chain owner who needs visibility across properties.
- Property manager/warden coordinating staff and tenants.
- Accountant or collection manager handling dues and receipts.

### 5.2 Secondary users

- Existing tenants who need payment, receipt, complaint, and stay workflows.
- Prospective tenants searching for appropriate accommodation.
- Staff assigned to housekeeping, maintenance, warden, or collection tasks.
- Platform operations and support personnel.

### 5.3 Beachhead

Coimbatore is the first operating cluster. The launch should concentrate on several dense education/employment corridors rather than spread thinly across the city. A cluster is considered viable when the company can service onboarding efficiently, generate useful marketplace choice, and create owner referrals within a short travel radius.

Expansion selection criteria:

- Concentration of colleges, technology/industrial employment, and migrant residents.
- Observable density of independent PG inventory.
- Owner willingness to digitize and pay.
- Local payment, language, support, and compliance readiness.
- Customer-acquisition cost and onboarding time demonstrated through a small field test.
- Marketplace liquidity: sufficient eligible beds and tenant demand in the same micro-markets.

- Tenants use the application without subscription fees.
- A time-bound trial reduces adoption friction.
- Plan limits are based on active properties/beds and capabilities, not hidden usage penalties.
- Marketplace visibility requires a valid subscription plus publication/moderation eligibility.

The base plan does not assume advertising revenue, data resale, or tenant subscription revenue.

## 8. Go-to-market strategy

### 8.1 Phase 1 — Design partners and proof (0–6 months)

- Recruit 5–10 design-partner PG owners in Coimbatore.
- Select a mix of single-property, multi-property, men's, women's, and unisex operations where legally/product appropriate.
- Digitize real workflows and measure time-to-first-property, time-to-first-bed, rent-record accuracy, and staff adoption.
- Offer high-touch onboarding in return for weekly feedback and permission to create anonymized case studies.
- Do not count unpaid design partners as paying traction.

### 8.2 Phase 2 — Cluster sales (6–18 months)

- Founder-led field sales in dense corridors.
- Owner referral program activated after successful first billing cycle.
- Demonstrations built around occupancy, collections, and complaint outcomes rather than feature volume.
- Partnerships with local accountants, property consultants, PG associations, facility vendors, and approved payment ecosystem partners.
- Tenant discovery campaigns limited to micro-markets with enough verified supply.
- Onboarding playbooks, templates, and imports reduce setup effort per bed/property.

### 8.3 Phase 3 — Repeatable expansion (18–36 months)

- Add city-launch pods only after Coimbatore unit economics and retention meet gates.
- Use inside sales for qualification and local partners/field teams for activation.
- Segment product and sales motions for small owners, growth operators, and chains.
- Create integrations/accounting exports that improve retention without becoming a custom-services company.
- Expand public marketplace only when listing freshness and response reliability can be maintained.

### 8.4 Sales funnel targets

| Stage | Initial target | Mature target |
|---|---:|---:|
| Qualified owner → product demonstration | 50% | 65% |
| Demonstration → trial | 35% | 50% |
| Trial → paid | 25% | 40% |
| Paid → live property within 14 days | 70% | 90% |

## 10. Defensibility

### 10.1 Operational system of record

Once inventory, tenants, invoices, payments, deposits, and complaints run through the system, the product becomes embedded in daily operations. Retention must come from measurable utility and safe data portability, not artificial lock-in.

### 10.2 Supply-demand data loop

Owner operations keep availability and pricing current. Better data improves tenant discovery and conversion. Tenant demand improves owner acquisition. More active owners improve marketplace coverage. This loop works only if data quality and trust are protected.

### 10.3 Localized workflow and distribution

Indian PG accommodation has local payment behavior, languages, document practices, deposit disputes, food/utility charges, and corridor-level demand. Productized local knowledge and repeatable city-launch playbooks are harder to reproduce than code alone.

### 10.4 Trust infrastructure

Tenant isolation, payment reconciliation, private KYC handling, scoped staff access, owner support, and auditable corrections can become a durable brand advantage in a category that handles sensitive identity and money data.

### 10.5 Data advantage with limits

Aggregated, privacy-preserving operational benchmarks may eventually help owners improve occupancy and collections. Personal data must never be sold, and tenant-level data must not be repurposed without a lawful, transparent basis.

## 12. Technology and operating model

### 12.1 Recommended product architecture

For this product, the recommended founder-stage implementation choice is:

- **FastAPI modular monolith** for business logic and versioned mobile/admin APIs.
- **Supabase managed PostgreSQL** with PostGIS for property discovery.
- **Supabase Auth** for identity, with application-owned organization memberships and authorization.
- **Supabase Storage** for property media and private documents with explicit RLS and separate buckets.
- Durable background workers/queues for invoice generation, webhooks, notifications, media scanning, and reconciliation.
- Flutter mobile application and a focused web administration console.

Supabase accelerates the pilot, but it does not remove the company's security responsibility. Every exposed table and storage path needs grants and Row Level Security. The secret/service role bypasses RLS and must never be shipped in the mobile/web client. Database backups do not automatically include Storage objects, so storage backup/recovery must be designed separately. [Supabase production checklist](https://supabase.com/docs/guides/deployment/going-into-prod), [RLS guidance](https://supabase.com/docs/guides/database/postgres/row-level-security), [database overview](https://supabase.com/docs/guides/database/overview).

### 12.2 Why not use Frappe as the core backend

Frappe is attractive for internal forms, DocTypes, workflows, permissions, and ERP-style back-office customization. It is a reasonable choice if the product is principally an ERP implementation for a few known operators.

BUDE HOME is instead intended to be a shared multi-tenant SaaS, mobile API, public geospatial marketplace, and high-integrity booking/payment system. FastAPI provides a cleaner boundary for explicit domain transactions, API contracts, concurrency controls, and independently designed mobile experiences. Frappe could still be used later for a separate internal operations tool or an ERPNext integration, but should not be the primary product backend.

### 12.3 Architecture principles investors should expect

- One deployable modular backend first; no premature microservices.
- PostgreSQL constraints and transactions prevent double booking and financial over-allocation.
- Organization context is derived from verified membership, not trusted from a client header.
- Issued invoices and confirmed financial events are immutable; corrections are auditable entries.
- Signed, duplicate-safe payment webhooks and daily reconciliation.
- Private KYC files, short-lived access, encryption, access audit, and retention/deletion workflows.
- Managed infrastructure, automated tests, observability, backup restore drills, and staged releases.

## 13. Security, privacy, and regulatory readiness

The product handles contact details, occupancy, identity documents, invoices, payments, deposits, complaints, and location information. Security and privacy are product requirements, not later compliance tasks.

### Control commitments

- Tenant isolation enforced in application policies and PostgreSQL RLS.
- Platform, organization, and property-scoped roles; support access is time-bound, approved, and audited.
- Multi-factor authentication for privileged users.
- Secure token storage on mobile; no secrets in shipped applications.
- Restricted data encryption, masking, private object storage, malware scanning, and short-lived file access.
- Payment provider handles card/payment entry; BUDE HOME minimizes PCI scope.
- Every payment webhook is signature-verified, deduplicated, order-independent, and reconciled.
- Structured logs exclude tokens, raw KYC, payment payloads, contact data, and signed file URLs.
- Secure development gates mapped to OWASP ASVS, API Security Top 10, and MASVS.
- Backup, point-in-time recovery, storage recovery, incident response, and independent security testing before broad GA.

India's Digital Personal Data Protection Act and notified Rules require a qualified legal implementation review. The company must confirm notices, lawful purpose/consent, processors, user rights, grievance handling, retention, security safeguards, breach response, children/guardian cases, and applicable commencement dates. The product must not claim legal compliance solely because technical controls exist.

## 15. Unit economics model

### 15.1 Mature target assumptions

| Metric | Target assumption |
|---|---:|
| Weighted monthly ARPA | ₹2,000–₹2,500 |
| Subscription gross margin | 80–88% |
| Blended gross margin | >75% |
| Monthly logo churn | <2.5% |
| Blended CAC | ≤₹12,000 |
| CAC payback | ≤8 months |
| Gross-margin LTV at ₹2,000 ARPA, 80% GM, 2.5% churn | Approximately ₹64,000 |
| Illustrative LTV/CAC | Approximately 5.3× |

Formula: gross-margin LTV = monthly ARPA × gross margin / monthly logo churn. This simple model excludes expansion, discount rate, and servicing changes; cohort contribution margin should replace it as data becomes available.

### 15.2 Unit-economics gates

- Do not scale paid acquisition until at least two cohorts show six-month retention.
- Do not open a new city pod until projected 12-month gross profit covers direct sales/onboarding/support cost.
- Do not subsidize tenant payments to manufacture volume.
- Track onboarding labor and messaging/payment costs inside gross margin, not hidden in general payroll.

## 19. Principal risks and mitigation

| Risk | Business impact | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|
| Owners resist paying or changing workflow | Low conversion/high churn | Design partners, outcome-based onboarding, lightweight plans, measure activation before scale |
| Informal inventory is difficult to verify | Marketplace trust loss | Subscription identity checks, publication checklist, moderation, recency/availability signals |
| Payment flow is not legally/provider approved | Delayed monetization/financial exposure | Subscription-first model; record offline tenant payments; launch fund movement only after approval |
| KYC/privacy incident | Severe trust/regulatory damage | Data minimization, encryption, RLS, restricted access, retention, audits, independent testing |
| Cross-tenant access defect | Existential SaaS risk | Application policies plus database RLS, two-tenant automated tests, incident stop gate |
| Double booking or incorrect balance | Owner/tenant loss and disputes | PostgreSQL constraints/transactions, immutable records, idempotency, daily reconciliation |
| Field onboarding cost is too high | Weak unit economics | Templates/imports, remote onboarding, partners, cluster density, explicit labor measurement |
| Marketplace cold start | Low tenant value | Operations SaaS works without marketplace; launch discovery only in supply-dense corridors |
| Incumbent or marketplace copies features | Pricing pressure | Deep workflow, local distribution, trusted data, integrations, service quality, brand |
| Premature multi-city expansion | Burn and inconsistent support | City gates tied to retention, CAC payback, density, and local owner capacity |
| Vendor concentration | Cost/outage/migration risk | Standard PostgreSQL, provider adapters, exports/backups, tested recovery, contractual review |
| Forecast underperformance | Runway risk | Monthly downside model, nine-month cash trigger, phase hiring, preserve core correctness/security |

## 23. Investment thesis in one paragraph

BUDE HOME CBE is an opportunity to build the operating and trust layer for a fragmented, recurring accommodation category: start with software that helps PG owners control beds, tenants, collections, deposits, and service; use that live operational data to create a more reliable tenant marketplace; and expand through dense city clusters with recurring owner-funded revenue. The value depends on disciplined execution—fast activation, provable retention, strong unit economics, payment correctness, and privacy—not merely on the size of India's accommodation market.
